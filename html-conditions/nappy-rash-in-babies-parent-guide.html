Nappy Rash in Babies

Gentle care for irritated skin in the nappy area, with signs that need medical advice.

Parent GuidePending clinician review

Draft review status

Prepared for clinical review on 9 September 2026. This draft is not included in the public resource index.

What does nappy rash look like?

The skin covered by the nappy may look red, sore, shiny, spotty, or feel warm. Mild rash often affects a small area and the baby otherwise seems well. Moisture, contact with urine or stool, friction, and irritating products can weaken the skin barrier.

Gentle care at home

  • Change wet or soiled nappies promptly and clean the area gently.
  • Use water and soft cotton or fragrance-free, alcohol-free wipes; avoid vigorous rubbing.
  • Pat the skin dry and allow nappy-free time when practical and safe.
  • Apply a thin layer of a simple protective barrier preparation recommended for babies.
  • Wash hands before and after every nappy change.

What to avoid

  • Do not use talcum powder, antiseptics, perfumed products, or harsh soap on irritated skin.
  • Do not use steroid, antifungal, antibiotic, or combination creams unless advised by a qualified clinician.
  • Do not scrub the rash or remove cream completely at every change if this further irritates the skin.

When to seek medical advice

Arrange assessment if the rash is severe, spreading, blistered, weeping, crusted, bleeding, involves open sores, affects skin folds, or is not improving with gentle care. Seek advice sooner if the baby has fever, seems unwell, feeds poorly, is unusually sleepy, or is under 3 months old and you are concerned.
