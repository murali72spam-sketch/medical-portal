Cradle Cap in Babies

Understanding common scalp scales in babies and caring for the scalp gently.

Parent GuidePending clinician review

Draft review status

Prepared for clinical review on 9 September 2026. This draft is not included in the public resource index.

What is cradle cap?

Cradle cap is a common form of infantile seborrhoeic dermatitis. It usually causes greasy or dry yellow-white scales on the scalp. It is not caused by poor hygiene, is not contagious, and often settles over time.

Gentle scalp care

  • Wash the scalp regularly with a mild, fragrance-free baby shampoo and rinse well.
  • Gently loosen soft scales with a soft baby brush; stop if the skin becomes sore.
  • If scales are thick, ask a healthcare professional or pharmacist about a baby-appropriate emollient and how to remove it safely.

What to avoid

  • Do not pick or scratch the scales; this can injure the skin and increase infection risk.
  • Do not use adult dandruff products, essential oils, fragranced oils, or medicated treatments unless a qualified clinician advises them.
  • Avoid leaving oily products on the scalp if they appear to worsen the scaling.

When to seek medical advice

Arrange assessment if the skin becomes very red, swollen, painful, weeping, crusted, or foul-smelling; if the rash spreads widely; if the baby seems unwell; or if the diagnosis is uncertain or gentle care is not helping.
