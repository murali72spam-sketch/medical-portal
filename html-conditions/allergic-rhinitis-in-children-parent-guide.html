Allergic Rhinitis in Children

Recognising persistent nasal allergy symptoms and planning practical care with your child's clinician.

Parent GuidePending clinician review

Draft review status

What is allergic rhinitis?

Allergic rhinitis is inflammation inside the nose caused by a reaction to an allergen. Symptoms may occur during particular seasons or throughout the year. Common triggers include pollens, house-dust mites, mould, and animal dander.

Common symptoms

  • Repeated sneezing and an itchy, runny, or blocked nose.
  • Itchy, red, or watery eyes and an itchy throat or palate.
  • Mouth breathing, disturbed sleep, tiredness, poor concentration, or frequent nose rubbing.

Fever, marked facial pain, a one-sided foul-smelling discharge, or a child who looks unwell may suggest another cause and should be assessed.

Practical care

  • Notice when symptoms occur and reduce confirmed or strongly suspected triggers where practical.
  • Avoid tobacco smoke, vaping aerosols, incense smoke, and strong fragrances around the child.
  • Saline nasal spray or rinse may help some children when used with clean equipment and safe water according to professional advice.
  • Discuss persistent symptoms with a clinician or pharmacist. Use antihistamines or nasal medicines only in an age-appropriate way and exactly as advised.

When to seek help

Arrange assessment if symptoms disrupt sleep, school, play, or breathing; persist despite appropriate care; or occur with recurrent wheeze, cough, snoring, ear or hearing problems. Seek urgent help for severe breathing difficulty, blue or pale colour, collapse, or swelling of the tongue or throat.
