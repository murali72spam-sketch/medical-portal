Oral Health and Toothbrushing in Children

Practical habits that help protect children’s teeth and gums from the first tooth onward.

Healthy habits begin early. Gentle daily cleaning, supervised brushing, sensible food and drink choices, and preventive dental care can help reduce tooth decay.

Before and after the first tooth

  • Before teeth appear, gently wipe the gums with a clean, soft, damp cloth.
  • Begin brushing as soon as the first tooth appears.
  • Choose a small, soft toothbrush appropriate for the child and replace it when the bristles become worn.
  • Make brushing part of a calm, predictable family routine.

A simple toothbrushing routine

  • Brush twice each day, including before bed.
  • An adult should brush or actively help and supervise until the child has good brushing skills.
  • Encourage the child to spit out toothpaste and avoid swallowing it.

Using fluoride toothpaste safely

Fluoride toothpaste helps prevent tooth decay. The appropriate product and amount can depend on the child’s age, individual risk, local guidance, and fluoride exposure. Follow advice from your child’s dentist or qualified healthcare professional and the product instructions. Keep toothpaste out of reach and supervise its use.

Food, drinks, bottles, and cups

  • How often a child has sugary foods and drinks matters. Keep sugary foods and drinks to mealtimes.
  • Offer water or plain milk rather than sugary drinks.
  • Do not put juice or sugary drinks in a baby bottle.
  • Do not give sugary snacks between meals or at bedtime.

Routine dental care

Arrange preventive dental care rather than waiting for pain. A dentist can check for early problems and give individualized brushing and fluoride advice. Seek dental review for pain, suspected tooth decay, a damaged tooth, or a tooth injury.

When urgent help is needed

Seek urgent dental assessment for facial or mouth swelling, suspected infection, uncontrolled bleeding, pain, or mouth or tooth trauma.
