Teething in Babies

What parents may notice, safe ways to comfort a baby, and symptoms that should not be assumed to be teething.

Teething is a normal part of development. Some babies have no obvious discomfort, while others have mild gum soreness, drooling, chewing, or fussiness for a short time.

What is teething?

Teething is the process of a baby’s teeth moving through the gums. The timing and sequence vary between children. Front teeth are usually among the first to appear.

What might parents notice?

  • A sore, tender, or slightly red gum where a tooth is appearing.
  • More drooling than usual and a mild rash around the mouth from wet skin.
  • Wanting to chew or gnaw on safe objects.
  • Mild fussiness or unsettled sleep.
  • No noticeable symptoms at all.

Safe ways to comfort your baby

  • Wash your hands, then gently massage the sore gum with a clean finger.
  • Offer a clean, purpose-made teething toy that is suitable for your baby’s age and used as directed.
  • A clean, cool cloth or cooled teething toy may feel soothing. Do not let your baby chew directly on an object that is frozen solid.

What to avoid

  • Do not use amber or other teething necklaces, bracelets, or anklets. They can cause choking or strangulation.
  • Do not use teething products containing benzocaine or lidocaine.
  • Do not use homeopathic teething tablets or gels, or apply alcohol to the gums.
  • Do not use a damaged or leaking liquid-filled teething toy.
  • Ask your child’s doctor or dentist before using medicines for teething discomfort.

Caring for the first teeth

Begin gentle brushing when the first tooth appears. Use an age-appropriate soft toothbrush and follow advice from your child’s dentist or healthcare professional about fluoride toothpaste. See the Oral Health and Toothbrushing in Children guide for everyday care.

Symptoms that may not be teething

Do not assume that fever or diarrhoea is caused by teething. If your baby seems unwell or has symptoms that concern you, seek medical advice rather than assuming teething is the cause.
