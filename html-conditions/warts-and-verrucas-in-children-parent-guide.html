Warts and Verrucas in Children

Recognising common viral warts, reducing spread, and knowing when to ask for help.

Parent GuidePending clinician review

Draft review status

What are warts and verrucas?

Warts are small, usually harmless skin growths caused by a virus. A verruca is a wart on the sole of the foot and may feel uncomfortable when standing or walking. They can look rough and may have tiny dark dots. Many clear without treatment, although this can take months or longer.

Reduce irritation and spread

  • Encourage the child not to pick, scratch, or bite a wart.
  • Keep the wart covered with a plaster during swimming and cover a verruca with a waterproof plaster.
  • Wear footwear in communal changing or shower areas and avoid sharing towels, socks, shoes, or nail tools.
  • Wash hands after touching a wart and avoid shaving over it.

Treatment choices

Treatment is not always necessary. If a wart is painful, persistent, or troublesome, ask a pharmacist or clinician which options are suitable for the child's age and the wart's location. Follow product instructions carefully; treatments can irritate healthy skin and may take time to work.

What to avoid

  • Do not cut, burn, puncture, or attempt to remove a wart at home.
  • Do not use wart treatments on the face or genitals, or on broken or infected skin, unless specifically advised by a clinician.
  • Do not assume every new lump is a wart if its appearance is unusual or changing.

When to seek medical advice

Arrange assessment if the growth is on the face or genitals; is painful, bleeding, infected, rapidly changing, or spreading extensively; the diagnosis is uncertain; the child has reduced immunity; or the wart is causing significant distress or difficulty walking.
