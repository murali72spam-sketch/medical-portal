Snoring and Obstructive Sleep Apnoea in Children

When regular snoring may signal obstructed breathing during sleep and deserves assessment.

Parent GuidePending clinician review

Draft review status

Prepared for clinical review on 9 September 2026. This draft is not included in the public resource index.

Snoring and sleep apnoea are not the same

Many children snore occasionally during a cold. Obstructive sleep apnoea occurs when the upper airway repeatedly narrows or closes during sleep, disturbing breathing and sleep. Enlarged tonsils or adenoids are common contributors; nasal allergy, obesity, craniofacial differences, and some neuromuscular or genetic conditions can also increase risk.

Signs to discuss with a clinician

  • Loud habitual snoring, especially on most nights.
  • Pauses, gasps, choking sounds, laboured breathing, or marked restlessness during sleep.
  • Persistent mouth breathing, unusual sleeping positions, sweating, or bedwetting.
  • Morning headaches, daytime sleepiness, irritability, hyperactivity, poor attention, learning concerns, or growth problems.

What parents can do

  • Record how often snoring occurs and note pauses, gasps, sleep position, and daytime symptoms.
  • A short sleep video can help a clinician understand what you observe, but it cannot diagnose or exclude sleep apnoea.
  • Keep the child's bedroom free from tobacco smoke and vaping aerosols.
  • Do not give sedating medicines or use an adult anti-snoring device unless a qualified clinician recommends it.

Assessment and treatment

A clinician may examine the nose, throat, tonsils, growth, and associated conditions. Some children need overnight monitoring or a sleep study. Treatment depends on the cause and severity and may involve treating nasal disease, healthy weight support where relevant, ENT assessment, surgery, or positive-airway-pressure therapy under specialist supervision.

When help is urgent

Seek urgent medical help if breathing pauses are prolonged or accompanied by blue or very pale colour, severe breathing effort, choking, difficulty waking, collapse, or a seizure.
